# Expanded Ancient Parallels and Guards Dissertation Audit v0.1

WP-OX-RES-0001 | 2026-10-08

## Aim and fair-comparison rule

Compare the *claims*, source proximity, transmission routes and event types. Neither discount ancient parallels as 'mere myths' without examination nor equate any disappearance story with the Christian claim of bodily resurrection. A primary ancient narrative about another person is not that person's preserved firsthand testimony.

## Comparator register

| Case | Ancient locus | Alleged phenomenon | Earliest surviving testimony in this audit | Material difference from Pauline evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jesus | 1 Cor 15:3–8; Gal 1 | Death, burial, raising, named appearance recipients; Paul personally reports an appearance | Paul's first-person epistles, c. 50s CE, roughly two decades after crucifixion | Earliest author reports his own experience; also transmits others' claims; latter are not separately preserved depositions |
| Aristeas of Proconnesus | Herodotus, Histories 4.14–15 | Reported death, missing body, conflicting sighting, reappearance seven years later; further manifestation tradition | Herodotus, fifth century BCE, reports what townspeople said | **Important close narrative parallel** for missing body and subsequent appearance; Herodotus does not claim to have seen Aristeas; original incident date uncertain |
| Cleomedes of Astypalaea | Pausanias, Description of Greece 6.9.6–8 | Man disappears from closed chest; Delphi pronounces him no longer mortal; hero cult follows | Pausanias, second century CE | Disappearance and exaltation without a reported bodily return from verified death; author relays tradition |
| Apollonius of Tyana | Philostratus, Life of Apollonius 8.29–31 | Multiple reported end-of-life traditions, ascension account and postmortem dream appearance to youth | Philostratus, early third century CE | Postmortem appearance is explicitly a dream in surviving account; underlying Damis memoir ends before the death traditions |
| Romulus | Plutarch, Romulus 27–28 | Disappearance and oath-supported manifestation to Proculus, followed by divine exaltation | Plutarch, early second century CE, drawing on older traditions | Oath testimony is *reported*, not extant Proculus first-person document; alleged events set centuries earlier |

## Direct primary-text observations

**Aristeas:** Herodotus 4.14 says the fuller reported Aristeas dead, another traveler contested this by reporting an encounter, and the body was absent when the room was opened. Herodotus then records later appearances; 4.15 explicitly distinguishes what townspeople say. This challenges any claim that an ancient combination of apparent death, absent body, and appearance was unique to Christianity. https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D4

**Cleomedes:** Pausanias 6.9.6–8 describes a violent offender hidden in a chest, subsequently absent, with an oracle granting heroic status. No verified death/resurrection sequence is described. https://periegesis.abm.uu.se/en/reports.php?reportid=180

**Apollonius:** Philostratus 8.29–31 explicitly presents uncertain end-of-life traditions; the youth's postmortem appearance is in a dream. The supposed Damis memoirs end before the death narrative (8.29), so they cannot be cited as a preserved eyewitness source for the dream. https://www.livius.org/sources/about/philostratus-life-of-apollonius/

**Romulus:** Plutarch, Romulus 27–28: the claim of a manifestation and an oath is not equivalent to a surviving sworn deposition by Proculus. Source: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Lives/Romulus%2A.html

## Full dissertation audit: Kankaanniemi (2010)

Matti Kankaanniemi, *The Guards of the Tomb (Matt 27:62–66 and 28:11–15): Matthew's Apologetic Legend Revisited*, Åbo Akademi University Press, 2010, full 353-page PDF inspected selectively at the relevant concluding sections:
https://doria.fi/bitstream/handle/10024/67076/kankaanniemi_matti.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1

**Exact printed-page findings:**

- **pp. 257–258:** He rejects most alleged historical implausibility arguments as decisive, but judges Matthew's guard-inflected tomb account difficult to harmonize with Mark's presentation. He argues Matthew probably combined a prior guard tradition with the Markan tomb account. This is a tradition-history argument, not proof of actual guards.
- **pp. 259–260:** He defends pre-Matthean guard material, an early empty-tomb tradition, and a longer conflict between the Jesus movement and opponents. These are his defended historical parameters, not independently established facts.
- **pp. 260–261:** He argues the body-theft accusation is likely old and cautions that Craig's inference from hostile accusation to an independently conceded empty tomb needs further elaboration.
- **pp. 261–262:** His distinctive reconstruction is that Jewish opponents may originally have introduced the *guards* in their counter-narrative. He explicitly acknowledges this is conjectural and admits an actual guard remains a historically possible alternative.
- **p. 262:** He suggests the developed Christian guard narrative was later than the basic tomb traditions, because those traditions do not contain guards.

**Critical warning:** Kankaanniemi's narrative reconstruction of what opponents actually said is not itself a surviving independent Jewish document. His argument is valuable because it explicitly resists an automatic late-Matthean-invention verdict, yet its specific scenario is not verified as historical fact.

## Provisional comparison

- **Absolute uniqueness of ancient death/disappearance/appearance claims:** not defensible; Aristeas provides a closer narrative analogue than previously recorded.
- **Comparable early documentary provenance:** the sampled comparators do not furnish an extant first-person letter from a person claiming an encounter on the model of Paul's testimony. This is a **sample-bounded** finding, not proof that no ancient comparator exists.
- **Distinct Christian evidentiary configuration:** early Pauline firsthand experience report + inherited named list + Gospel tomb narratives. Source dependence limits how much these may be multiplied.
- **No inference to supernatural causation yet:** even an unusually early documentary configuration does not alone select bodily resurrection over early sincere experience and transmission.
