# Matthew, Justin, and Gospel of Peter: Textual Dependence Audit

WP-OX-RES-0001 | 2026-10-08

## Question

Does the theft accusation's repetition in Justin and the guard narrative in the Gospel of Peter establish independent adversarial evidence for an empty tomb?

## Matthew and Justin

**Matthew 28:13 (Greek):** λέγοντες, Εἴπατε ὅτι Οἱ μαθηταὶ αὐτοῦ νυκτὸς ἐλθόντες ἔκλεψαν αὐτὸν ἡμῶν κοιμωμένων. The core allegation is that the disciples came at night and stole him while the guards slept. Source: https://www.greekbible.com/matthew/28/13

**Justin, Dialogue with Trypho 108:** Justin alleges that Jewish representatives spread the message that Jesus' disciples stole him from the tomb by night and falsely proclaimed his resurrection and ascension. Source: https://readtheoria.com/read/dialogue-with-trypho/chapter-108

**Observed overlap:** the specific combination *disciples + theft + at night* appears in both, rather than merely a generic missing-body explanation. Justin also specifies the tomb and juxtaposes theft with false resurrection proclamation. Matthew adds sleeping guards, priestly payments, and continued dissemination; Justin adds a worldwide delegation accusation.

**Text-critical restraint:** The overlap above is confirmed at the level of Matthew's Greek and Justin's English translation. Justin's Greek has **not** been collated word-for-word against a critical Greek edition in this increment. Do not assert a proven shared Greek phrase or precise verbal dependence without that check.

**Independence verdict:** Shared motifs support continuity of an accusation, but they do not determine whether Justin knew Matthew, relied on a shared polemical tradition, or observed an independently persisting opponent claim. Justin's attribution to hostile agents is an *assertion in a Christian polemical text*, not a surviving hostile document.

## Gospel of Peter, sections 8–11

The surviving narrative greatly expands the tomb watch: soldiers observe radiant figures descending, an opening tomb, and figures emerging, with a speaking cross. This is not simply a copy of Matthew's guards sleeping while theft supposedly occurs. The different narrative design, however, is not itself proof of an independent historical guard tradition.

**Competing scholarship:**

- Craig, 'The Guard at the Tomb', argues the very limited verbal overlap with Matthew permits an independent guard tradition. Source: https://www.reasonablefaith.org/writings/scholarly-writings/historical-jesus/the-guard-at-the-tomb
- Paul Foster, *The Gospel of Peter: Introduction, Critical Edition and Commentary*, argues a substantial literary relationship with canonical Gospel traditions, including Matthew's distinctive guard material. Source excerpt: https://www.scribd.com/document/721954655/Foster-Gospel-of-Peter-Introduction-Critical-Edition-and-Commentary
- Kankaanniemi, *The Guards of the Tomb* (2010), notes the debate: Craig emphasizes little verbal similarity, whereas Raymond Brown identifies similarities suggesting knowledge of Matthew; Kankaanniemi cautions that the fragment is too short for confident vocabulary statistics. Source: https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/39937924.pdf
- Loke, *Investigating the Resurrection of Jesus Christ*, chapter on Jesus' physical body, notes both canonical-dependence and earlier-tradition possibilities and addresses the differing commands to guards. Source: https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/master/gdc/gdcebookspublic/20/20/02/21/28/2020022128.pdf

**Dependency verdict:** The Gospel of Peter is a later surviving witness to the guard narrative. Its dramatic additions are evidence of narrative development, not corroboration that guards observed a resurrection. Its precise relationship to Matthew remains contested; absence of extensive exact wording does not by itself establish independence, especially where narrative motifs align.

## Historical inference tree

1. Matthew reports an alleged theft counterclaim. **Directly attested as a Matthean report.**
2. Justin later repeats the allegation and attributes it to Jewish representatives. **Directly attested as Justin's report.**
3. A theft controversy existed before Matthew. **Plausible hypothesis, not independently established by Justin alone.**
4. Actual informed opponents accepted a missing body. **Conditional on (3) and on the accuracy of the attributed counterclaim.**
5. The burial place was historically empty. **Requires additional tomb and burial evidence.**
6. Jesus was bodily raised. **Not entailed by (5); must integrate independent appearance and causal evidence.**

Each step requires its own warrant. Neither affirmative nor skeptical models may collapse multiple steps into one.

## Findings

- **Continuity:** The theft accusation is attested in Matthew and repeated by Justin in a distinct second-century Christian work.
- **Independent hostile attestation:** Still **not demonstrated**.
- **Guard historicity:** Neither Gospel of Peter's elaboration nor Justin's repetition resolves it.
- **Historical value:** The body-theft tradition is an important test case for early polemical interaction; its probative value increases if pre-Matthean hostile provenance can be demonstrated independently.
- **Pauline independence:** The first-generation appearance proclamation and living-500 assertion remain analytically distinct from the later tomb-polemic stream.
