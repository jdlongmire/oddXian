# Distinctive Resurrection Narratives and Ancient Comparator Audit v0.1

WP-OX-RES-0001 | 2026-10-08

## Question

Do Matthew's guards, Luke's Emmaus narrative and John's Thomas narrative supply independently grounded historical evidence for resurrection, or do they chiefly demonstrate the development and transmission of resurrection claims? Compare with a genuinely similar ancient postmortem appearance claim, not only with mundane events.

## Passage-specific ledger

| Account | Direct textual claim | Independent attestation of the specific episode | Historically discriminating potential | Principal limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Matthew 27:62–66; 28:11–15 | Authorities post guards; guards later report and are bribed to circulate theft allegation | Guard/bribery story unique to Matthew among canonical Gospels; Gospel of Peter later preserves guard motif, dependence disputed | Could reflect early adversarial dispute over disposition of body | Narrative's polemical function and proposed pre-Matthean source do not verify guard deployment or the opponent's original words |
| Luke 24:13–35 | Two travelers encounter Jesus, initially fail to recognize him, recognize him at meal | Episode specific to Luke; underlying source unverified | Distinct appearance context, recognition and vanishing | No separately preserved firsthand deposition by either traveler; literary function substantial |
| John 20:24–29 | Thomas doubts, then encounters Jesus with wounds; Jesus invites inspection | Thomas episode unique to John; underlying source unverified | Strong explicit bodily continuity claim | Text reports invitation and Thomas's response; it does not explicitly say Thomas physically touched wounds |
| Luke 24:12,24 / John 20:3–10 | Peter and others inspect tomb | Two literary narratives, with literary dependence/shared source disputed | Potential convergence on an inspection tradition | Two texts are not necessarily independent source streams |
| Plutarch, Romulus 27–28 | Julius Proculus swears Romulus appeared after disappearance, now glorified | Surviving report from Plutarch and earlier Roman tradition; not extant first-person Proculus deposition | Genuine analogue for postmortem glorified appearance and oath testimony | Alleged event centuries before extant narrative; Romulus's historicity contested; apotheosis differs from Jesus' bodily resurrection |

## Scholarly adversarial audit

**Matthew's guards:** Craig, 'The Guard at the Tomb', concedes serious objections (uniqueness and the leaders' understanding of predictions) but argues that underlying Jewish-Christian polemic may be early; his own discussion ultimately calls guard historicity an open question. Source: https://www.reasonablefaith.org/writings/scholarly-writings/historical-jesus/the-guard-at-the-tomb

Kankaanniemi, M. (2010), *The Guards of the Tomb (Matt 27:62–66 and 28:11–15): Matthew's Apologetic Legend Revisited*, Åbo Akademi University Press, argues for a pre-Matthean dispute over theft and treats the historical guard question as distinguishable from the polemical tradition. Dissertation record and summary: https://doria.fi/handle/10024/67076 . Full dissertation not yet inspected page by page.

**Peter's inspection:** Craig, 'The Disciples' Inspection of the Empty Tomb', surveys direct literary dependence and common-tradition explanations: https://www.reasonablefaith.org/writings/scholarly-writings/historical-jesus/the-disciples-inspection-of-the-empty-tomb/ . A 2025 *Novum Testamentum* article, 'An Archimedean Point for Dating the Gospels', argues for Luke's literary knowledge of John, using Luke 24:12,24 as evidence. That is a contested proposal, not a settled chronology: https://doi.org/10.1163/15685365-bja10101 .

**Visionary interpretation:** A 2026 Harvard Center for the Study of World Religions essay, 'Jesus of Nazareth', argues that nonordinary appearance features in Emmaus and John fit visionary interpretation. These are authorial arguments; unusual features may also be consistent with a *transformed bodily resurrection* hypothesis, so neither interpretation follows from strangeness alone: https://cswr.hds.harvard.edu/news/2026/07/15/jesus-nazareth .

**Ancient comparator:** Plutarch, *Romulus* 27–28, directly reports the disappearance, murder accusation and Proculus's sworn encounter with a glorified Romulus: https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Plutarch/Lives/Romulus%2A.html . It rebuts an absolute claim that antiquity lacked comparable *appearance stories*, not a narrower claim about comparable **source proximity and documentary provenance**.

## Dependency-adjusted units

- **U1:** Pauline direct testimony to Paul's own claimed encounter.
- **U2:** Pauline received appearance list, provenance partly overlapping early Christian community.
- **U3:** Markan tomb proclamation and inherited Synoptic narrative complex.
- **U4:** Matthew's guard controversy as a *single* Matthean stream, with possible pre-Matthean polemical antecedent.
- **U5:** Lukan Emmaus episode as one Lukan literary stream, underlying source unknown.
- **U6:** Johannine Thomas episode as one Johannine literary stream, underlying source unknown.
- **U7:** Luke/John tomb inspection as a *potentially shared* stream pending source-critical adjudication.

These are accounting units, not independent eyewitnesses or independent probability factors. Independence must be demonstrated before multiplying likelihood ratios.

## Preliminary parity judgment

1. **Claim-level uniqueness:** Rejected in absolute form. Romulus supplies a meaningful ancient postmortem-appearance comparator.
2. **Documentary profile uniqueness:** Still open. Paul supplies earlier surviving authorial testimony to his own experience than Plutarch supplies for Proculus; comparison with Apollonius and other ancient cases remains necessary.
3. **Bodily continuity:** Luke and John make increasingly explicit claims about tangible wounds and eating; these are literary data and potential historical traditions, not separately corroborated tactile examinations.
4. **Guard argument:** Pre-Matthean controversy is plausible but cannot establish an actual guard, theft attempt, or independently documented concession by opponents.
5. **Emmaus and Thomas:** Potentially important distinctive traditions, but presently **unverified independence**; no likelihood increment assigned solely for distinctiveness.
