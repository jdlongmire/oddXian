## Conventions

**D** = direct first-person claim by an extant author; **R** = author reports a person/group or event; **N** = narrative depiction of an event, without a separately surviving witness statement; **C** = external corroboration of the specific proposition; **—** = not attested in the passage. These codes are **descriptions**, not numeric weights. **R/N** cannot be converted to multiple independent witnesses by counting named characters. Literary dependence is tracked separately.

## Interpretation and dependency controls

1. **Paul's first-person experience:** 1 Corinthians 15:8 is a first-person claim; the earlier appearance list is reported testimony. Do not count each listed recipient as an extant firsthand source.
2. **Mark's shorter ending:** Mark 16:1–8 reports an empty tomb and a promised future appearance, but depicts no completed appearance. Mark 16:9–20 is text-critically disputed and excluded from the early Mark column.
3. **Synoptic overlap:** Matthew and Luke share material and likely literary dependence on Mark under the dominant Markan-priority hypothesis. Do not assign them independent-evidence multipliers without passage-level source criticism.
4. **John:** Distinct narrative content does not prove total source independence. Investigate relationship to Synoptic and pre-Gospel traditions.
5. **Josephus/Tacitus:** External corroboration applies to the specific historical anchor stated; neither corroborates the objective resurrection.
6. **Proposition granularity:** 'An author says X', 'X was believed', 'X occurred', and 'God caused X' are distinct propositions with potentially different probabilities.

## Comparative assessment design

Build one row per *proposition*, not per whole book or religion. For each source record: earliest surviving textual witness; estimated composition interval (with disputed ranges); first-person vs reported; named purported witness; independence from other accounts; external corroboration; text-critical caveats; potential counterevidence; and historiographical acceptance with cited scholarly arguments. The current matrix is qualitative and cannot justify a posterior probability.
