# Competing Hypotheses Matrix v0.1

WP-OX-RES-0001 | 2026-10-08 | Research stage: qualitative, no fabricated likelihoods

## Evidentiary propositions

E1: Jesus was executed by crucifixion.
E2: Paul personally claimed an experience he understood as an appearance of risen Jesus.
E3: Paul transmitted a received tradition naming Cephas, the Twelve, more than 500, James, and others.
E4: Early Christians proclaimed Jesus' resurrection.
E5: Gospel texts narrate an empty tomb and appearances, with debated historical independence and factual reliability.
E6: James became a prominent Christian leader, and Paul reports meeting him.

**Caution:** E2–E4 share historical transmission pathways. E5 may depend on overlapping oral/literary traditions. Do not multiply their probabilities as independent observations.

## Candidate explanations

| Hypothesis | Explanatory opportunities | Main challenges | Critical discriminator |
|---|---|---|---|
| H-R: Jesus was bodily raised and appeared | Accounts for early appearance claims, their bodily-resurrection interpretation, and later narratives under a unified cause | Extraordinary causal claim; independent evidence for physical event disputed; divine causation requires additional inference | Objective bodily encounters independently attested and strongly resistant to alternative mechanisms |
| H-V: Individual visionary / revelatory experiences | Directly accommodates Paul's experience report and some appearance traditions; known class of religious experience | Need to account for varied group claims, specific resurrection interpretation, and narrative evolution without presupposing all were identical experiences | Evidence of collective experiences versus reports of them; nature of earliest experience claims |
| H-S: Socially mediated experiences and interpretive convergence | Explains community-level reinforcement, shared proclamation, and elaboration of traditions | Cannot simply assume all group encounters are social contagion; needs historically grounded mechanism and timeline | Independent attestation and phenomenology of purported group appearances |
| H-L: Legendary accretion upon an early proclamation | Can explain expansion and variation of later narrative details | Cannot explain away Paul's early personal claim merely by dating Gospels later; early core remains | Which specific details demonstrably arise later, and when |
| H-F: Deliberate fabrication | Could produce intentional claims and narratives | Requires evidence of intentional deception and coordinated incentives; Paul as former opponent is a difficulty | Positive evidence of invention, motive, and collusion |
| H-M: Mistaken identification / survival / misperception | Appeals to ordinary mechanisms | Must be specified precisely; survival must overcome execution evidence; mistaken identity alone does not cover all claims | Independently supported circumstances enabling error |
| H-C: Combination of natural mechanisms | Can assign different causes to different strands of evidence, avoiding one-size-fits-all explanation | Increased flexibility risks ad hoc accommodation and overfitting; complexity must be penalized consistently | Independently motivated component mechanisms and predictive constraints |

## Qualitative adjudication rules

For every E_i and H_j, record whether the evidence is expected, compatible, surprising, or contradictory *conditional on source dependence and survival selection*. These are qualitative judgments until justified likelihood intervals exist. Explicitly distinguish likelihood P(E|H) from posterior P(H|E). A hypothesis that explains the reports does not thereby establish the reported event. A resurrection hypothesis must also distinguish the claim of bodily rising from the additional inference that God raised Jesus.

## Guardrails against asymmetric skepticism

No hypothesis wins by worldview designation, number of proponents, or rhetorical simplicity. For each hypothesis apply the same demands for mechanism specification, source fidelity, explanatory scope, independence, testable expectations, and defeaters. Neither a naturalistic nor a supernatural hypothesis receives a zero prior by fiat in the main comparison. Report a zero-prior naturalism scenario only as a separate philosophical constraint, not as an empirical finding.
