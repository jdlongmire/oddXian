## Source-independence graph, conceptual

- **Paul's own claimed experience** (1 Cor 15:8; Gal 1:11–16) is one authorial stream across two letters, not two independent eyewitnesses.
- **Paul's received appearance tradition** (1 Cor 15:3–7) is a reported stream whose original transmitters and formation date are debated.
- **Mark → Matthew/Luke** represents the conventional Markan-priority model for shared literary material. Specific resurrection episodes require passage-level analysis; unique material does not automatically equal an independent eyewitness.
- **John** may preserve additional traditions, but the degree of independence from Synoptic or shared oral traditions remains open.
- **Tacitus** independently writes as a Roman historian; independence of his *underlying information* from Christian reports is not established simply by author identity.
- **Josephus** supports the identification of James as Jesus' brother but supplies no appearance attestation.
- **Romulus and Apollonius** each depend, for the specified appearance proposition in this sample, on later surviving literary transmitters, not extant testimony by the purported seers.

## Bayesian design

Let R = Jesus bodily rose, H_i = a specified rival explanation, and E = the observed surviving evidence (not the alleged event itself).

Posterior odds:
`P(R | E) / P(H_i | E) = [P(R) / P(H_i)] × [P(E | R) / P(E | H_i)]`

Do **not** multiply individual witness likelihood ratios as if independent. Factor the evidence conditionally:
`P(E_1,...,E_n | H) = P(E_1 | H) × P(E_2 | E_1,H) × ... × P(E_n | E_1,...,E_{n-1},H)`.

### Scenario dimensions, without invented numbers

1. **Prior assumption:** strict metaphysical naturalism (R assigned zero prior, thus untestable by ordinary Bayesian updating); methodological naturalism without metaphysical exclusion (small nonzero prior); broad theism; Christianity-specific background assumptions. A zero prior is a model stipulation, not an empirical result.
2. **Tradition independence:** maximal dependence; moderate shared tradition; stronger independent provenance. Test how conclusions change.
3. **Experience mechanisms:** genuine bodily appearance; individual visionary experiences; collective socially mediated interpretation; legendary expansion; deliberate invention; combinations.
4. **Transmission fidelity:** high, medium, low fidelity, justified from source criticism rather than assigned arbitrarily.
5. **Selection and preservation:** survival bias, unknown lost counter-testimony, community incentives, and the fact that records were selected for preservation.

### What would count as an inductive advantage?

The Christian resurrection evidence favors R over a rival H_i when the *joint pattern of surviving testimony and corroboration* is more expected under R than under H_i, after dependence and selection effects. An exceptional documentary pattern does not automatically imply an exceptional event. The result can vary with the rival specified and the prior chosen.

### Falsification / sensitivity checkpoints

- A credible earlier non-Christian parallel with comparable first-person testimony would weaken claims of documentary uniqueness.
- Demonstrated dependence among purportedly separate appearance traditions reduces cumulative evidentiary weight.
- If reasonable prior ranges dominate any plausible likelihood advantage, posterior conclusions should be reported as prior-sensitive.
- If rival explanations explain the observed early proclamation and its development comparably well, an exclusive best-explanation claim fails.
- Historians' differing treatment of ordinary versus supernatural claims constitutes an evidentiary-parity problem only when matched propositions and defensible background rates are controlled.
