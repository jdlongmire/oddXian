# RESEARCH ROADMAP: Resurrection as a Comparative Inductive Historical Argument

Status: Active research campaign | Version 1.0 | 2026-10-08
Owner: JD Longmire | Repository: jdlongmire/oddxian

## Research objective
Assess whether the resurrection proclamation has a historically distinctive evidentiary profile and whether the cumulative evidence discriminates bodily resurrection from specified alternatives, using symmetrical historical standards.

## Rules of engagement
- Scripture and theological commitments may inform worldview-level interpretation; historical-source claims must still be evaluated by explicit historical criteria.
- Distinguish what a source reports, what happened, and which causal explanation best accounts for it.
- Count independent sources, not repetitions; account for literary and oral dependence.
- The 500 are one early Pauline report of a simultaneous group appearance, not 500 preserved depositions.
- Distinguish acceptance by a community from independent verification, and no surviving rebuttal from no dispute.
- Apply identical standards to Christian and non-Christian comparators; neither naturalistic nor supernatural hypotheses get free assumptions.
- Do not invent numerical probabilities, quotations, Greek collations, citations, or completed verification.
- Use Harvard author–year citations, primary-source loci, explicit uncertainty and a change log.
- Commit coherent batches; reserve user decisions for material thesis changes, scope changes, or publication approval.

## Phase sequence and deliverables

### Phase 1. Baseline integration
Inventory existing WP files; deduplicate findings; create claim-to-source IDs, issue ledger, and a single evidence baseline. Reconcile contradictory judgments and identify unverified citations.
**Exit:** each central claim has provenance, confidence category and an unresolved-questions entry.

### Phase 2. Primary sources, chronology and independence
Audit Pauline passages, pre-Pauline tradition, canonical Gospel literary relationships, Greek semantics, burial traditions, non-Christian references, and documentary dating. Distinguish manuscript dating from composition and tradition dating.
**Exit:** source-dependency graph and dated evidence matrix, with contested ranges explicit.

### Phase 3. Witnesses, reception and contestability
Evaluate Cephas, James, Paul, the Twelve, 500, group-appearance reporting, audience reception, travel/access, expected challenge and document survival. Test the rhetoric of living witnesses against ancient historiography and correspondence.
**Exit:** reception/contestability dossier and conditional silence model.

### Phase 4. Adversarial evidence and tomb polemic
Complete Matthew 28–Justin Dialogue 108 critical Greek collation; Gospel of Peter dependence; guard historicity; body-theft polemic; Celsus via Origen; earliest independently preserved hostile evidence.
**Exit:** hostile-attestation register separating independent hostile documents from Christian reports of hostile claims.

### Phase 5. Comparative ancient parity
Pre-register comparison class and criteria. Include Aristeas, Cleomedes, Romulus, Apollonius, and additional analogues selected without outcome bias. Compare death evidence, firsthand authorship, source proximity, group claims, circulation, reception, accessibility, independent testimony, corporeality and challenge.
**Exit:** bounded parity matrix and uniqueness claims qualified to sample.

### Phase 6. Explanatory models
Specify resurrection (R), early visionary/social transmission (C), legend development (L), fraud (F), and composite models. Separate explanatory fit to early belief, group report, burial/tomb and later elaboration. Avoid multiplying dependent likelihoods.
**Exit:** conditional evidence ledger, sensitivity table and model-specific predictions.

### Phase 7. Red-team and robustness
Strongest affirmative, skeptical and agnostic arguments assessed by quality, not ideological headcount. Re-run findings under early/late dates, dependent/independent sources, strong/weak empty-tomb support and varied prior commitments.
**Exit:** red-team report, counterarguments and surviving conclusions.

### Phase 8. Synthesis and publication
Write technical paper, public-facing argument, executive evidence matrix, source bibliography, and limitations appendix. Separate historical conclusions from theological and metaphysical interpretation.
**Exit:** reviewed, publication-ready package, pending author approval.

## Checkpoints
A. Evidence baseline (phases 1–2).
B. Historical discrimination (phases 3–5).
C. Explanatory evaluation (phases 6–7).
D. Publication (phase 8).

## Execution protocol
Work sequentially by phase, but group several connected research tasks per commit. Maintain the tracker and append decision records when judgments change. Do not stop after each sub-question. Report at checkpoint boundaries, not every file. Continue until a genuine blocker requires a decision, or the current execution window ends; never imply asynchronous work without a scheduled mechanism.

## Existing corpus
The existing primary-source, dependence, comparator, likelihood, Pauline, guards, circulation and polemic audits are inputs. They are provisional research notes, not verified final conclusions. See the tracker for migration and acceptance checks.

## Thesis under test
The distinctive case, if established, concerns the conjunction of early firsthand Pauline testimony, inherited named-recipient tradition, a large simultaneous group-appearance claim with surviving-witness assertion, circulation to an identifiable community, and subsequent bodily-resurrection and tomb traditions. Establishing documentary distinctiveness is not identical to establishing the event's supernatural cause.
