# Semantic Corpus and Creed-Boundary Audit v0.1

WP-OX-RES-0001 | 2026-10-08

## Scope and method

This increment audits ὤφθη + dative in 1 Corinthians 15:5–8 against an accessible full scholarly article and separates the chronology of the *core formula* from the chronology of the *extended appearance list*. It does not assign numerical probabilities or favor a scholar because of worldview affiliation.

## Directly inspectable lexical study

Gieniusz, A.M. (2019), 'Jesus’ Resurrection Appearances in 1 Cor 15,5–8 in the Light of the Syntagma ὤφθη + Dative', *The Biblical Annals* 9(3), pp. 481–492, DOI 10.31743/biban.4526. Full PDF: https://czasopisma.kul.pl/index.php/ba/article/download/4526/4623/15022

- **pp. 482–485:** Gieniusz analyzes the passive form in non-Jewish Greek and Jewish-Greek corpora. His argument concerns manifestation/visibility, not automatic certification of a witness's sensory accuracy.
- **pp. 485–488:** He surveys Septuagintal and related Jewish uses, including divine manifestations. On p. 487 he distinguishes the formula's use for theophanies from ordinary prophetic-vision descriptions, with a noted textual exception.
- **pp. 487–491:** He examines 1 Cor 15, distinguishing the semantic range of the formula from Paul's particular claim. On p. 490 he explicitly allows that ordinary visual perception is *not excluded* by the expression. He argues the reported appearance to more than five hundred would be difficult to construe as simultaneous ecstasy.
- **pp. 491–492:** His conclusion emphasizes the eschatological/salvific function of the formula and rejects using the phrase by itself as proof of the factuality of appearances.

**Critical synthesis:** The study cannot responsibly be reduced to 'ὤφθη means vision'. Its argument is that the Greek construction by itself cannot authenticate the event. Equally, it does not rule out normal perception, and its discussion of the 500 is potentially relevant to an evidentiary discriminator. Whether the 500 actually had a shared encounter remains independently unverified.

## Creed boundaries

Moffitt, D.M. (2008), 'Affirming the “Creed”: The Extent of Paul's Citation of an Early Christian Formula in 1 Cor 15,3b–7', *Zeitschrift für die neutestamentliche Wissenschaft* 99, pp. 49–73, DOI 10.1515/ZNTW.2008.004. Publisher abstract: https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/ZNTW.2008.004/html . Institutional bibliography: https://research-portal.st-andrews.ac.uk/en/publications/affirming-the-creed-the-extent-of-pauls-citation-of-an-early-chri/

The accessible abstract documents a live dispute over whether vv. 5 and 7 reflect traditions associated with competing Petrine and Jacobean groups, rather than a single original fixed formula. **The full article was not inspected.** Do not attribute Moffitt's detailed final conclusion or precise argument to him on the basis of its title.

| Portion | Secure conclusion | Remaining question |
|---|---|---|
| 15:3–5 | Paul identifies received material including death, burial, resurrection, and Cephas appearance | How early was its exact wording stabilized? |
| 15:6 | Paul reports more than 500 at once | Did this item belong to the earliest received formulation? |
| 15:7 | Paul reports appearance to James and apostles | Independent tradition, later addition, or integral formula? |
| 15:8 | Paul directly places his own claimed experience last | Paul's first-person report does not establish the phenomenology of all earlier encounters |

**Dating discipline:** The letter provides a terminus ante quem for the reported material. 'Pre-Pauline' is not a precise year. A proposed early dating must state whether it applies to 3–5, 3–7, or the entire list.

## James evidence ladder

1. **Firsthand Paul:** Galatians 1:19 reports meeting James, identified as the Lord's brother; 1 Corinthians 15:7 reports an appearance to James.
2. **Independent historical identity:** Josephus, *Antiquities* 20.9.1, names James as Jesus' brother; does not discuss a resurrection appearance.
3. **Later Gospel narrative:** John 7:5 says Jesus' brothers did not believe in him during his ministry; this is collective narrative testimony, not James's autobiographical admission.
4. **Inference:** 'James changed his mind because of an objectively bodily encounter' is an explanatory hypothesis, not an independently attested confession by James.

## Adversarial inference check

**Against simplistic visionary reduction:** ὤφθη does not entail a merely internal or disembodied experience; Paul's larger bodily-resurrection argument and Gieniusz's own treatment of group appearances require careful attention.

**Against simplistic physical proof:** ὤφθη does not by itself establish objective bodily contact, and Paul's list is not equivalent to independent testimony from all listed recipients.

**Against late-legend-only models:** Paul's firsthand encounter claim and the appearance tradition were already present in his first-century correspondence; the question is not whether they originated only in the late Gospel narratives.

**Against overconfident early dating:** The date of the core received formula and the date of the whole appearance catalogue are different claims and require separate support.
