# Scholarly Source-Criticism Protocol and Initial Bibliography v0.1

WP-OX-RES-0001 | 2026-10-08

## Epistemic parity policy

Do not weight an author's testimony or argument according to Christian, skeptical, or noncommittal identity. Evaluate primary-source fidelity, explicit premises, chronology, dependency analysis, alternative hypotheses, and methodological consistency. Record worldview priors explicitly where they affect inference. A consensus assertion must be supported by an identifiable survey and definition of its sample; otherwise mark it unverified. Do not use equal headcounts as a substitute for assessing arguments.

## Argument parity test

For each contested proposition, construct two strongest competing arguments and document:
1. Ancient source locus and original-language/text-critical issue.
2. Author's reconstruction and its premises.
3. Evidentiary contribution distinct from prior source streams.
4. Rival's strongest objection in the rival's own published work.
5. Response and unresolved defeaters.
6. The precise claim justified: report, belief, historical occurrence, or divine causation.

**Illustrative tension:** Licona's historical hypothesis testing and Ehrman's visionary-experience proposal must each explain Paul's testimony, the received appearance list, and later narrative development. A hypothesis's conformity with a scholar's metaphysical preference does not count as independent historical evidence. Nor does the fact that a hypothesis is supernatural exempt it from ordinary explanatory scrutiny.
