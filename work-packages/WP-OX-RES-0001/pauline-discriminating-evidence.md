# Discriminating Evidence: Pauline Tradition, Paul, and James (v0.1)

WP-OX-RES-0001 | 2026-10-08 | Provisional source-critical research

## Research question

Which elements of the earliest surviving resurrection testimony discriminate between a bodily resurrection (R) and a specified composite alternative (C), rather than merely showing that resurrection was proclaimed?

## Direct ancient textual anchors

**1 Corinthians 15:3–8.** Paul states he delivered what he received: Christ died, was buried, was raised on the third day, and appeared to Cephas, the Twelve, more than five hundred at once, James, all the apostles, and last to Paul. The repeated 'appeared' language links Paul's reported experience to the list, but does not establish identical phenomenology. The passage is direct evidence of Paul's reporting and transmitting a tradition; it is not separately preserved testimony by each recipient. Primary text: https://www.esv.org/1+Corinthians+15/

**Galatians 1:11–24.** Paul presents his gospel as revelatory, acknowledges earlier persecution of the church, and reports meeting Cephas and James. This is firsthand autobiographical testimony to Paul's self-description and claimed contact. It does not say that James described an appearance to Paul or specify Paul's sensory experience. Primary text: https://www.esv.org/Galatians+1/

**Galatians 2:1–10.** Paul reports subsequent interaction with Jerusalem leaders. This supports his claim of direct engagement with prominent members of the movement, but does not independently verify the resurrection. Primary text: https://www.esv.org/Galatians+2/

**Josephus, Antiquities 20.9.1.** Identifies James as brother of Jesus called Christ and reports his execution. Independent support for James's historical identity and prominence in Jerusalem, not for James's reported appearance. Primary text: https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=J.+AJ+20.9.1

## Chronology and provenance constraints

1. 1 Corinthians is commonly dated c. 53–55 CE; the underlying received material must predate Paul's delivery, but the **exact number of years** between crucifixion and formulation is disputed.
2. Paul does not explicitly identify the original composer of the complete list, its location of composition, or the date each item entered the tradition. A dating of the entire list to within months of crucifixion cannot be assumed.
3. Paul's meeting with Cephas and James increases the plausibility that he could consult early leaders. Access to a person is not evidence of the precise information exchanged.
4. Josephus's independent reference corroborates James's existence and relationship to Jesus; it is not independent attestation of James's conversion mechanism.

## Paired explanatory challenge

| Observed source datum | Bodily resurrection R | Composite naturalistic C | What would discriminate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paul claims an encounter after prior opposition | Direct appearance can account for a sudden reversal | A revelatory/visionary experience plus reinterpretation can account for reversal | Specific sensory, temporal, and interpersonal details not supplied by the extant firsthand claim |
| Paul reports Cephas and James among recipients | A common objective referent could explain diverse recipients | Multiple experiences and subsequent shared tradition could produce list | Independent early statements from Cephas/James, or independently corroborated shared encounter details |
| Paul reports group appearances | R could produce shared observation | C requires group-report formation or multiple coordinated experiences; mechanism must be specified | Independent first-person group testimony, detail convergence, and documented provenance |
| Paul knows Cephas and James | Allows cross-checking in principle | Also expected if Paul joined and consulted the movement | Evidence that a specific check occurred and what was verified |
| James leads Jerusalem movement | Consistent with a transformative appearance | Consistent with several possible conversion and leadership trajectories | Earliest independently attested motive and timing of James's change |

## Adversarial checks

**Strongest objection to R:** The evidence is testimony about experiences and tradition, with no direct independently preserved access to the physical event. A supernatural causal explanation can be made flexible by unspecified divine intentions. R must avoid post hoc auxiliary assumptions.

**Strongest objection to C:** An unspecified mixture of vision, social reinforcement and literary development can be adjusted to fit nearly any report. C must provide independently motivated mechanisms, plausible timing, and constrained predictions, especially for Paul's pre-conversion opposition and early group claims.

**Crucial distinction:** The absence of a documented natural mechanism in this specific case does not establish R; likewise, the possibility of natural mechanisms does not establish C.

## Falsifiable research tasks

- Locate earliest textual witnesses and critical editions of 1 Corinthians 15 and Galatians; separate manuscript date from composition date.
- Compare scholarly dating of the creed/formula, specifying whether each author dates verses 3–5, 3–7, or 3–8.
- Compare whether 'appeared' (ōphthē) denotes an objective encounter, visionary experience, or is semantically compatible with both in contemporaneous Greek usage.
- Establish the earliest attestations of James's skepticism and conversion, separating Gospel narrative, Acts, Josephus, and later church tradition.
- Identify the strongest independently documented model for Paul's conversion under R and C, and specify prospective discriminators.

## Provisional conclusion

The surviving Pauline record gives strong grounds for an early resurrection proclamation and Paul's own asserted encounter, plus a direct personal connection to Cephas and James. These features are potentially discriminating, especially relative to purely late-legend models. They do not yet yield a justified likelihood ratio between bodily resurrection and a well-specified visionary/composite alternative.

No worldview camp receives privileged weighting; the burden of mechanism specification and independent corroboration applies symmetrically.
