## First comparative findings (provisional)

1. Paul's source is unusually consequential because a participant in the early movement personally asserts an appearance and names other appearance recipients. Its number of named or grouped recipients is not the number of independent extant sources.
2. Josephus supplies an independent anchor for James's identity and death, without attesting an appearance.
3. The Romulus tradition is a serious counterexample to claims that ancient cultures lacked postmortem appearance or ascension narratives. Whether it matches the *evidentiary structure* of the Christian accounts remains unproven.
4. A claim that the resurrection has no ancient historical equivalent is **not established** by this initial sample.
5. Acceptance of Caesar's assassination cannot be compared to acceptance of Jesus' bodily resurrection without separating the underlying historical claims, evidence for them, and causal background probabilities.

## Evidence comparison dimensions

For each proposition, score or narratively document: temporal distance between event and extant witness; first-person versus reported testimony; number of independent source streams; named and anonymous persons; corroboration; transmission; hostile or nonaligned attestation; historical context; competing explanations; survival bias; and probability sensitivity to worldview priors.

**Do not** assign numerical probabilities before developing a transparent model and source-dependency graph.
