# Earliest Resurrection Objections and Adversarial Attestation Audit

WP-OX-RES-0001 | 2026-10-08 | Research increment

## Question

What are the earliest *surviving* objections to the Christian resurrection proclamation? Do they independently corroborate an empty tomb, appearances, public reception, or the contemporaneous accessibility of the 500?

## Chronological evidence register

## Key primary-text observations

### Matthew 28:11–15

Matthew presents a theft explanation attributed to guards, passed through priests, and reportedly circulated to Matthew's own time. This is evidence that *Matthew represents* an opposing explanation as circulating. If that representation preserves a genuine dispute, it would suggest opponents addressed a missing-body claim rather than merely denying appearances. Yet the historical existence, antiquity, and independence of the reported opposition are separate hypotheses. Mark does not preserve the guards episode. Kankaanniemi (2010) offers a pre-Matthean polemic reconstruction while acknowledging conjecture.

### Justin, Dialogue with Trypho 108

Justin alleges opponents publicly claimed the disciples stole the corpse and falsely announced resurrection. His statement is later than Matthew and could be dependent on Matthew or shared Christian polemic. Justin does not supply an original named Jewish document, direct quotation from a known opponent, or a dated first-century record of a tomb inspection. Therefore it cannot be counted as a second independent hostile eyewitness attestation without additional argument.

Primary text: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/01287.htm

### Celsus in Origen, Against Celsus 2.55–63

Celsus's reported critique invokes other miraculous-return narratives, raises doubts about whether anyone truly dead returned bodily, challenges the appearance witnesses, and proposes dreams, wishful imagination, or deception. His challenge that Jesus did not appear publicly to hostile authorities is especially relevant to *contestability*. These are genuine ancient objections, but their preserved form is late and mediated through Origen. Celsus is not known to have interviewed the 500 or produced a surviving contemporary refutation of 1 Cor 15:6.

Origen explicitly quotes the 500 passage in 2.63 while addressing Celsus's challenge. This documents third-century Christian use of the passage, **not** independent confirmation of the group encounter.

Primary text: https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/04162.htm

### Tertullian, Apology 21 and De Spectaculis 30

Tertullian records theft accusations and the alternative of a gardener removing the body. The latter appears within rhetoric and cannot simply be classified as a formal hostile historical hypothesis with known proponents. Neither text supplies independent verification of Matthew's guards.

Primary texts:
- https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0301.htm
- https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0303.htm

## Dependence and counting rule

The sequence Matthew → Justin → Tertullian may represent a continuing dispute, but it is **not automatically three independent corroborations** of an actual empty tomb. Source dependence, literary borrowing, and shared apologetic tradition must be tested. Likewise, Celsus's hostile critique establishes opposition to the claim in the late second century; it does not demonstrate direct access to the original witnesses.

## Adversarial-attestation matrix

| Proposition | Earliest relevant evidence | What can be concluded | What cannot yet be concluded |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jesus' resurrection was preached early | Paul, 1 Cor 15 | Early proclamation and community reception | Truth of event |
| Group of 500 allegedly saw Jesus | Paul, 1 Cor 15:6 | Specific early report and living-witness assertion | 500 preserved testimonies or actual verification |
| Some Christians contested resurrection doctrine | Paul, 1 Cor 15:12 | Internal doctrinal disagreement | Denial of the 500 specifically |
| A theft counterclaim circulated | Matthew, then Justin | Attested Christian reports of such a counterclaim | A surviving first-century hostile witness or independently verified empty tomb |
| Skeptics proposed vision/deception explanations | Celsus via Origen | Explicit late-second-century counter-explanations | That Celsus knew what the 500 individually reported |
| No surviving direct rebuttal of the 500 is identified | Current examined corpus | Absence in bounded extant record | Universal silence, acceptance, or confirmation |

## Historical significance

The adversarial record does **not** show an unchallenged resurrection proclamation. It shows that the proclamation generated different kinds of challenge: doctrinal denial, alleged body theft, questions about appearances, and objections to the limited audience of appearances.

A potentially important discriminator remains the **chronological asymmetry**: Paul's report about living group witnesses survives from the first generation; the extant literary presentation of explicit hostile alternative explanations comes later. That asymmetry increases the importance of Paul's letter as evidence of what was being claimed early. It does not establish that the claim was independently tested or uncontested in the intervening years.

## Next tests

1. Compare Matthew's theft tradition and Justin's language for literary dependence, with original-language controls.
2. Examine Kankaanniemi's proposed origin of guards polemic alongside alternatives that treat Matthew as apologetic composition.
3. Catalogue pre-Celsus anti-Christian testimony, separating mere rejection of Christianity from direct resurrection counterclaims.
4. Investigate what the earliest opponents *could reasonably have known* about the 500, including anonymity, geography, language and travel.
5. Model missing rebuttal evidence using explicit conditional stages: formulation, communication, recording, copying and survival.
6. Distinguish rebuttal of a *missing-body claim* from rebuttal of *physical resurrection*; neither automatically settles the other.

## Methodological conclusion

A fair argument should neither assert that enemies 'admitted the empty tomb' solely from Matthew/Justin nor discount the early and unusually concrete Pauline witness-accessibility claim. The proper result is a constrained evidentiary ledger: the early proclamation is secure; the 500 group report is early and specific; its independent confirmation is not preserved; and identifiable objections appear in later, differently mediated texts.
